## Stage A: graph ring and centered algebra

`GraphRing.lean` packages arbitrary finite simple undirected graphs using an actual
finite vertex type and a Mathlib `SimpleGraph`. Empty and disconnected graphs are
included. Disjoint union is Mathlib's graph sum; multiplication is its Cartesian
`boxProd`, not its tensor/direct product.

`GraphRing` is the free commutative ring on these graphs, quotiented by the ideal
generated by exactly the empty, K₁, isomorphism, sum and Cartesian-product
relations. `liftInvariant` and `graphRing_hom_ext` prove the universal ring-valued
invariant extension property and uniqueness. No walk or symbol identity is a
presentation relation. `epsilon` descends vertex count through every relation;
in particular it maps K₁ to 1, so the presentation is not the zero ring.

`Augmentation.lean` proves:

- Actual graph classes span the ring **additively** over ℤ. This is stronger than
  ring generation and uses the Cartesian-product relation.
- `center x = x - epsilon(x)`, `centered G = [G] - |V(G)|[K₁]`, and
  `augmentationIdeal = ker epsilon`.
- The centered actual graphs span this ideal additively.
- For **positive** q, `(I^q).restrictScalars ℤ` is the additive span of products of
  exactly q centered actual graph classes. The q=0 boundary is deliberately
  excluded: the empty product alone spans ℤ·1, while I⁰ is the whole ring.
- `vanishes_on_power_iff` transfers vanishing on all such products to every
  virtual graph-ring element in I^q, for any ℤ-linear invariant.

`CenteredSupport.lean` proves the centered inclusion–exclusion expansion **in the
constructed ring**, its evaluation under a ℤ-linear map, and pointwise support
cancellation. T denotes removed coordinates; its weight is
`(-1)^|T| product_{i in T} |V(G_i)|`. The empty-subset term is retained; dropping
it requires a separately proved invariant value at K₁. `support_cancellation`
cancels one supported object, and `full_support_extraction` exchanges the finite
sums to extract exactly full support for any finite object family.

## Stage B: arbitrary-factor trajectories and exact multiplicity

`CartesianWalks.lean` defines cyclic sequences of actual dependent-tuples of
factor vertices. Each step includes an adjacency-checked coordinate witness,
proved uniquely recoverable from the trajectory. Both closure and the NB seam
are part of the cyclic indexing. None of the definitions presupposes double
occurrence, edge confinement, or an allowed Word.

`no_single_change` proves by an exact integer-indicator telescoping sum that a
function on a finite permutation cannot change at just one place. Applied to
Cartesian coordinates it proves every used coordinate occurs at least twice,
so `full_support_budget` gives `2q ≤ m` for any full-support length-m walk, and
`full_support_empty` excludes all such walks when `m < 2q`. This is an actual-walk
bound, still distinct from a statement about the graph-ring invariant on I^q.

At length 2k, `product_maximal_twice` forces every coordinate to occur twice.
`maximal_factor_edge` then confines its actual vertex projection to the two
endpoints of a factor edge. `maximal_axis_no_repeat` proves that consecutive
equal coordinates would reverse that edge; this includes last–first adjacency.
`productMaximalWord` admits the recovered coordinates to the existing `Word k`
type. It does not repeat the word/matching proofs or assume their hypotheses.

`FactorMultiplicity.lean` supplies actual forward realization, uniquely
recoverable oriented edges, and uniqueness of an actual trajectory with those
edges and the given word. `fullSupportEdgeBijection` establishes

`FullProductWalk G ≃ (Π i, FactorArc (G i)) × Word k`.

The first endpoint of each arc is the initial coordinate of the walk. Thus
oriented arcs already count the 2^k starting-endpoint choices: there is no extra
2^k multiplier on this bijection. Mathlib's independently established dart/edge
cardinality and the merged `word_count` yield `full_support_factor_count`:

`card (FullProductWalk G) = beta k * product_i |E(G_i)|`.

This is the exact all-k factor multiplicity at the level of actual walks.
At this stage the graph-ring global symbol still required additive count descent and
fiber correspondence listed in Stage A obligations 3–4. No conditional assembly
lemma is substituted for those missing proofs.

The independent `oracles/graph_ring_support.py` imports no repository oracle. It
compares explicit vertex DFS with sparse arc-matrix traces; centered sums with
full-support sets; and recovered oriented-edge/word tuples with independently
enumerated Cartesian products of those data, checking injectivity, surjectivity,
and actual trajectory reconstruction. Fixtures include nonregular bipartite,
nonbipartite, disconnected, isolated-vertex, edgeless and empty factors. Negative
controls show the uncentered Q₃ length-four count 48 differs from its centered
defect 32, and a square with a leaf has two linearly NB length-six closed walks
but no cyclically NB one. The checked-in receipt is bounded computational evidence.

## Stage C: actual count descent and concrete product classes

`GraphRig.lean` constructs finite graph isomorphism classes as a commutative
semiring, deriving distributivity, unit and empty laws from actual vertex
isomorphisms. `AdditiveLift.lean` proves every additive integer invariant of
these classes extends uniquely to `GraphRing`. Its proof evaluates free-ring
monomials in that rig, extends by the free abelian group, and proves every
presentation relation is annihilated **after every ring multiplier**, before
quotient descent. Thus nonmultiplicative counts are not passed to the
ring-homomorphism universal property by mistake.

`WalkInvariant.lean` defines `NBWalk` as actual cyclic vertex trajectories,
without coordinate witnesses. It proves isomorphism invariance and a bijection
between walks in a disjoint union and the disjoint union of the two walk sets.
The tag of a vertex is constant around the walk, so no trajectory can switch
summands. `nbInvariant m` is the resulting ℤ-linear invariant on virtual graph
classes, with its actual graph evaluation proved in `nbInvariant_class`.

`CartesianRing.lean` proves the actual dependent Cartesian tuple graph has
class equal to the product of the factor classes, including the zero-factor
K₁ case. `DiscreteGraphs.lean` proves an edgeless graph is its vertex count
copies of K₁ in the ring. These are structural graph results, with no NB symbol
claim assumed. The final Stage D assembly below identifies coordinate masking with
actual walk restriction and applies the checked support cancellation and
ideal-power spanning. The global theorem is not inferred from finite cardinal
experiments.


## Stage D: formal global filtration and symbol

`MaskedSupport.lean` establishes the exact missing correspondence. Replacing a
factor's edges by the empty relation retains its actual vertices. The checked
`maskedWalkEquiv` identifies its product walks with precisely the unmasked walks
whose coordinate sequence never uses that factor. Step adjacency and cyclic NB,
including last–first, are preserved in both directions.

The edgeless class identity retains all fixed-coordinate vertex choices, including
zero choices for empty factors. No division, nonemptiness, regularity,
connectedness or bipartiteness is introduced. `masked_count` expresses each masked
count by per-walk support indicators. Together with the constructed additive
invariant, centered ring expansion and pointwise cancellation, this yields

`nb_centered_full_support : b_m(product_i u_Gi) = card(full-support ProductWalk G m)`.

`EdgeInvariant.lean` constructs the ℤ-linear unoriented edge count `edgeInvariant`
on the same ring. Isomorphism invariance and disjoint-union additivity are proved;
its value at K₁ is zero, and centering preserves its value.

`NonbacktrackingSymbol.lean` proves these statements separately:

| Declaration | Kernel-checked statement |
|---|---|
| `nb_upper_filtration` | For positive q and positive m with m < 2q, `b_m(I^q)=0`, on arbitrary virtual elements. |
| `nb_graph_symbol` | For k≥2, the actual-graph k-fold centered defect is `beta k * product_i |E(G_i)|`. |
| `multilinear_graph_ext` | Equality on actual graph classes extends to all virtual factors, using the proved additive spanning in each coordinate. |
| `nb_global_symbol` | `centeredSymbol k b_(2k) = beta k • edgeTensor k`, an equality of multilinear maps on the entire ring. |
| `nb_global_symbol_apply` | Pointwise formula for arbitrary virtual factors `x_i`: `b_(2k)(product_i center x_i) = beta k * product_i d(x_i)`. |
| `nb_exact_order` | `b_(2k)` vanishes on I^(k+1) but does not vanish on I^k. The nonzero witness uses K₂ and the existing `beta_pos`. |

Here `centeredSymbol` has **no factorial divisor**. Positive-length typeclass
hypotheses `NeZero m` and `NeZero (k+k)` merely let Lean use cyclic `Fin` addition;
k≥2 guarantees the latter and introduces no extra mathematical restriction.
`HasExactAugmentationOrder` explicitly distinguishes the upper bound from failure
of vanishing on I^k. No primitive or aggregate-deficit assertion is used in its proof.

`Hashimoto.lean` additionally proves `nbHashimotoBijection` between actual vertex
trajectories and rooted closed trajectories on the directed-arc Hashimoto
transition relation, with its cyclic seam. Thus the formal count has the intended
rooted oriented NB semantics. Expansion of matrix powers to express the same count
as a literal matrix trace is not formalized here; that standard matrix encoding is
separate from the proved graph-ring identity.
